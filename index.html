How it works

Start narrow. Prove value. Expand only if it works.

We begin with one repetitive ticket category, so your team can judge quality, speed and effort saved without changing your support operation overnight.

  1. 01

    Choose one repetitive ticket category

    Onboarding questions, integration setup, configuration issues or recurring how-to requests. We pick the one that repeats most.

  2. 02

    MitraLabs drafts the responses

    We use your existing support content and the way your team has answered before to prepare a draft for each ticket in that category.

  3. 03

    Your team reviews and sends

    Agents approve, edit or discard each draft. We track what they changed and report weekly on what is actually worth automating.

The 30-day pilot

A low-risk way to find out whether this helps your team

The pilot is narrow and measurable on purpose. One support category, thirty days, and a clear picture at the end of where repetitive work can realistically be reduced.

  • Scoping for one repetitive support-ticket category
  • AI-assisted draft replies built on your existing knowledge base
  • A human-reviewed workflow, so your team approves what is sent
  • A weekly insight report with volume, patterns and opportunities
  • An end-of-pilot review to decide whether to continue, expand or stop

Trust and data handling

Early conversations depend on trust. We keep the pilot narrow, work with the minimum access necessary and stay transparent about how information is handled.

    Pilot access is limited to what the chosen category needs.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you replace our support team?

No. We prepare drafts for repetitive tickets and your agents decide what gets sent. The aim is to give them time back for complex customer issues.

Do we need to change our help desk?

No. The pilot runs alongside the help desk and process you use today. We agree the exact setup together on the intro call.

Do you automate all ticket types?

No. We start with one repetitive category and only suggest expanding where the weekly reports show quality is consistently strong.

What happens after the pilot?

We review the thirty days together and you decide whether to continue, expand to another category or stop. There is no obligation to carry on.
